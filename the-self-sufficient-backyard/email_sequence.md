## Email 2
**Subject:** The number one reason small gardens underperform

Most small backyard gardens do not fail because of bad soil or bad luck. They fail because of one planning mistake: cramming too many crops into too little space without a layout.

When everything competes for the same light and root space, everything underperforms together. A garden planned around square-foot yield instead of fitting it all in routinely produces two to three times more food in the same footprint.

This is mistake one of seven I see over and over. Tomorrow I will cover the second one, it is about timing, not space.

## Email 3
**Subject:** Self-sufficient on a quarter acre? Here is what that really means

Self-sufficient sounds like an all-or-nothing homestead thing. It is not.

On a quarter acre or less, it looks like layers stacking on top of each other:

- A vegetable garden sized to your actual eating habits, not Pinterest
- A couple of fruit trees or berry bushes tucked into unused corners
- Maybe a few hens if your area allows it
- Preserving the surplus so nothing rots on the vine

Each layer chips away at your grocery bill. None of them require acreage, they require sequencing, which is the part almost nobody plans for.
